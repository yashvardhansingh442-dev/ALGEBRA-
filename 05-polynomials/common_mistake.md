# Common Mistakes — Polynomials

## 1. Only flipping the sign of the first term when subtracting

When subtracting one polynomial from another, the negative sign must distribute across **every** term in the second polynomial — not just the first one.

Wrong: `(5x² - 3x + 1) - (2x² + 4x - 6)` → `5x² - 3x + 1 - 2x² + 4x - 6` ❌ (only flipped the first term's sign)
Right: `5x² - 3x + 1 - 2x² - 4x + 6` ✓ (every term in the second polynomial got flipped)

This is the same distribute-the-negative trap from Module 02, just applied to a polynomial with more terms — more terms means more chances to miss one.

## 2. Combining terms that aren't actually "like" terms

Only terms with the exact same variable and exact same exponent can be combined. `3x²` and `5x` are **not** like terms, even though they both contain `x`.

Wrong: `3x² + 5x = 8x²` ❌ or `8x` ❌ (these can't be combined at all)
Right: `3x² + 5x` stays as two separate terms — there's nothing to simplify

## 3. Forgetting to add exponents when multiplying variables

When multiplying `x` terms together, the exponents **add** — they don't multiply and don't stay the same.

Wrong: `x² · x³ = x⁶` ❌ (multiplied the exponents) or `x² · x³ = x²` ❌ (ignored the second exponent)
Right: `x² · x³ = x²⁺³ = x⁵` ✓

## 4. Only multiplying two of the four FOIL pairs

FOIL requires four separate multiplications (First, Outer, Inner, Last). A very common shortcut-gone-wrong is multiplying only the First and Last terms and skipping Outer and Inner entirely.

Wrong: `(x + 3)(x - 5) = x² - 15` ❌ (skipped Outer and Inner)
Right: `(x + 3)(x - 5) = x² - 5x + 3x - 15 = x² - 2x - 15` ✓

## 5. Treating (x + 5)² as x² + 25

Squaring a binomial is **not** the same as squaring each term separately. `(x + 5)²` means `(x + 5)(x + 5)`, which needs full FOIL — the middle terms don't disappear.

Wrong: `(x + 5)² = x² + 25` ❌
Right: `(x + 5)² = (x + 5)(x + 5) = x² + 10x + 25` ✓ (the middle term is real and easy to forget)

## 6. Not distributing a monomial across every term of a longer polynomial

When multiplying a monomial by a trinomial (or longer), every single term needs the monomial distributed to it — it's easy to distribute to the first two terms and forget the last one, especially with negative signs involved.
