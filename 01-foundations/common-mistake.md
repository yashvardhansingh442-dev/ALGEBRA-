# Common Mistakes — Foundations

These are the errors that show up most often. Reading this before doing the practice set will save you from repeating them.

## 1. Treating M before D and A before S as strict rules

PEMDAS is often memorized as a strict left-to-right hierarchy, but **multiplication and division are the same priority tier**, and so are **addition and subtraction**. Within a tier, work left to right.

Wrong: `20 ÷ 4 × 5` → doing multiplication first gives `20 ÷ 20 = 1` ❌
Right: left to right → `20 ÷ 4 = 5`, then `5 × 5 = 25` ✓

## 2. Mishandling `-x²` vs `(-x)²`

`-2²` is **not** the same as `(-2)²`.

- `-2²` means "the negative of 2 squared" → `-(2²) = -4`
- `(-2)²` means "negative 2, squared" → `(-2) × (-2) = 4`

The parentheses change everything. Always check whether the negative sign is inside or outside the exponent.

## 3. Forgetting to distribute a negative sign across parentheses

`-(x - 3)` is **not** `-x - 3`. The negative sign flips the sign of *every* term inside:

`-(x - 3) = -x + 3`

Skipping this step is one of the most common algebra errors at every level, not just for beginners.

## 4. Doing an operation to only one side of the equation

The golden rule is: whatever you do to one side, you must do to the other. A very common slip:

Wrong: `x + 5 = 12` → `x = 12` (forgot to subtract 5 from the right side too) ❌
Right: `x + 5 - 5 = 12 - 5` → `x = 7` ✓

## 5. Sign errors when dividing by a negative number

Dividing (or multiplying) both sides of an inequality or equation by a negative number is easy to fumble. For equations, the sign of the *variable's* coefficient just needs to be tracked carefully — for inequalities (covered in Module 03), dividing by a negative also **flips the inequality sign**, which is a separate and very common trap later on.

## 6. Rushing the "same sign / different sign" rule for multiplication

`(-3) × (-4)` and `(-3) × 4` look similar but give very different results (`12` vs `-12`). When in doubt, say the rule out loud: same signs → positive, different signs → negative.
