# Common Mistakes — Factoring

## 1. Skipping the GCF step

The most common reason a factoring problem "won't work" is that a GCF was sitting in plain sight and never got pulled out. `2x² - 18` looks stuck until you notice the `2`: `2(x² - 9)`, which then factors cleanly as `2(x - 3)(x + 3)`.

**Always check for a GCF before anything else.**

## 2. Stopping before the expression is fully factored

After pulling out a GCF or splitting into factors, check whether any remaining piece can factor again. `2(x² - 9)` is not finished — `x² - 9` is still a difference of squares.

## 3. Sign errors when picking the two numbers

For `x² - x - 12`, the numbers must multiply to `-12` **and** add to `-1`. A common slip is finding numbers with the right product but the wrong sum — `4` and `-3` multiply to `-12` but add to `+1`, not `-1`. The correct pair is `-4` and `3`.

**Quick check:** after picking your numbers, verify *both* the product and the sum before writing the factors.

## 4. Trying to factor a sum of squares

`x² - 9` factors as `(x - 3)(x + 3)`, but `x² + 9` does **not** factor over the real numbers. The difference-of-squares pattern only works with subtraction between the two squares.

## 5. Forgetting the GCF in the final answer

When you factor out a GCF and then factor what's left, the GCF has to stay in the final answer.

Wrong: `2x² - 18 = (x - 3)(x + 3)` ❌ (dropped the 2)
Right: `2x² - 18 = 2(x - 3)(x + 3)` ✓

## 6. Skipping the multiply-back check

Factoring is the one skill where you can always verify yourself. Multiply your factors back out with FOIL or distribution — if you don't get the original expression, something's off. This takes ten seconds and catches nearly every error above.

## 7. Confusing factoring with solving

Factoring an expression like `x² - 9` gives you `(x - 3)(x + 3)` — an **expression**, not an answer for `x`. There's no `= 0` yet, so there's nothing to solve. Solving factored equations comes in Module 09.
