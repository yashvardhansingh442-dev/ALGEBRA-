# Code of Conduct

## Our purpose

**ALGEBRA-** exists to help people learn algebra and to build the tools that teach it. Many of the people who use this repo are beginners, students, or self-learners who may feel unsure about asking questions or sharing work. This Code of Conduct exists so that everyone, at every level, feels safe doing both.

It applies to everyone who takes part in the project: maintainers, contributors, learners, and anyone opening an issue, pull request, or discussion.

---

## What we expect

**Be kind and patient.**
Every expert was once a beginner. Answer questions without making the asker feel small. "I don't understand this step" is a valuable thing to say, not something to be embarrassed about.

**Be respectful of different backgrounds and skill levels.**
People here come from different countries, schools, languages, ages, and levels of experience. Do not assume what someone should already know.

**Give and accept feedback gracefully.**
- Critique the work, not the person.
- Be specific and constructive: say what is wrong *and* how it could be better.
- When receiving feedback, assume good intent and take time to consider it before responding.

**Be honest about mistakes, including your own.**
Math and code both contain errors. Report them plainly, fix them without blame, and thank the person who found them. Mistakes in lessons are bugs, not failures of character.

**Credit other people's work.**
If you use a solution, explanation, or problem from another source, say where it came from and make sure you are allowed to share it. Do not copy copyrighted textbook content, exam questions, or solution manuals into the repo.

**Keep discussions on topic and useful.**
Issues and pull requests are for the project: lessons, code, and documentation. Keep them focused so people can find what they need.

---

## What we do not accept

The following behavior is unacceptable in any project space:

- Harassment, insults, mockery, or demeaning comments, including about someone's skill level, accent, writing, or questions
- Discrimination or hostile comments about a person's race, ethnicity, nationality, religion, gender, gender identity, sexual orientation, disability, age, appearance, or background
- Threats, intimidation, or encouraging harm to anyone
- Sexual language or imagery, or unwelcome sexual attention
- Sharing someone's private information, such as their address, email, or school, without their permission
- Sustained disruption: repeatedly derailing threads, spamming, or advertising unrelated products or services
- Deliberately submitting false, misleading, or plagiarized work
- Pressuring anyone to do their homework for them or to share exam answers. Learning together is welcome; cheating is not
- Any other conduct that would reasonably be considered inappropriate in a respectful learning community

**A note on learners and exams.** This repo is for understanding, not for evading assessment. Contributors are free to explain methods and worked examples, but are not expected to complete someone's graded assignment, and may decline to.

## Scope

This Code of Conduct applies in all spaces managed by the project, including:

- Issues, pull requests, discussions, and code review comments
- Commit messages and documentation
- Any official project channel or communication

It also applies when someone is representing the project in public, for example when posting under the project's name or presenting it.

## Enforcement

Maintainers are responsible for clarifying and enforcing these standards. They may remove, edit, or reject comments, commits, code, issues, and other contributions that do not follow this Code of Conduct, and will explain the reason when it is appropriate to do so.

Responses are meant to be proportionate. Depending on the situation, they may include:

1. **A private reminder.** For a first, minor, or clearly unintentional issue: a quiet note explaining what was inappropriate and why.
2. **A public or private warning.** For a more serious issue or a repeated one: a clear warning that continuing will lead to further action.
3. **A temporary restriction.** A time-limited ban from commenting, opening issues, or contributing.
4. **A permanent ban.** For serious violations, a pattern of violations, harassment, or threats.

Maintainers will aim to act fairly and consistently, will listen to everyone involved where possible, and will avoid acting on rumor alone. Decisions are made in the interest of keeping the community safe and welcoming.

---

## If you are not sure

If you are unsure whether something is okay, it is fine to ask a maintainer privately first. It is also fine to say "that comment felt unkind" directly, calmly, and without escalating, and to give the other person a chance to respond.
