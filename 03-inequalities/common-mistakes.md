# Common Mistakes — Inequalities

## 1. Forgetting to flip the sign

This is, by far, the single most common inequality mistake. Any time you multiply **or** divide both sides by a negative number, the inequality sign must flip.

Wrong: `-2x < 8` → `x < -4` ❌ (forgot to flip)
Right: `-2x < 8` → `x > -4` ✓

**A quick way to catch yourself:** if your last step was "divide by a negative," stop and check the sign before writing your final answer.

## 2. Flipping the sign when it's not needed

The flip rule only applies to multiplying/dividing by a **negative**. Adding or subtracting a negative number — or multiplying/dividing by a positive — never flips the sign.

Wrong: `x - 5 > 3` → `x < 8` ❌ (subtracting -5 doesn't need a flip; this step doesn't even involve multiplication/division)
Right: `x - 5 > 3` → add 5 → `x > 8` ✓

## 3. Losing track of which direction the inequality points after rearranging

When variable terms end up on the right side instead of the left, it's easy to accidentally reverse the inequality just from rewriting it. `3 < x` and `x > 3` mean the exact same thing — but writing `3 < x` as `x < 3` (flipping the variable's position without flipping the sign) is wrong.

If `a < b`, then `b > a` — the direction of the "opening" always points at the same value regardless of which side it's written on.

## 4. Treating compound inequalities as two separate, disconnected problems

For `-3 < 2x - 1 ≤ 9`, every operation must be applied to **all three parts** at once, not just the middle. Skipping the left or right part when adding/dividing leads to two inequalities that no longer describe the same range.

## 5. Mixing up open vs. closed circles when graphing

`<` and `>` get an **open** circle (the boundary value itself doesn't satisfy the inequality). `≤` and `≥` get a **closed/filled** circle (the boundary value does satisfy it). Mixing these up is a very common graphing error, especially under time pressure.

## 6. Assuming the solution is a single number

Unlike most linear equations, inequality solutions are usually a *range* — infinitely many values, not one. If your final answer looks like `x = 5` instead of `x > 5` or `x ≤ 5`, double check you didn't drop the inequality sign somewhere in your work.
